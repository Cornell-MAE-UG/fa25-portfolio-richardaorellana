In my Mechatronics class (MAE3780), my group was tasked with designing and building a robot to compete in the ASML Cube Craze competition. The design process was guided by very open-ended milestones which we had to complete before a deadline as a sign of progress on our robot. My group's final design was built entirely off the outline of the default chassis that everyone was provided. A red acyrlic arm acted as our collection apparatus.

While many sensors were provided, my team chose to forego using them and instead relied entirely on pre-programming the movement path of our robot. While this worked perfectly during testing, our robot did not perform well in the actual competition. An Arduino UNO acts as the brains of the robot and to it we have connected three motors. Two DC motors drive plastic wheels with rubber band tires, allowing for tank-style movement. The third motor is a PWM mini servo motor that kept the arm raised until it reached the color boundary on the board.

Although our team went 0-2-3 (wins-losses-ties), we do not consider our robot to be a failure. The lack of a feature that made it back away from opponents proved itself effective by practically disarming every robot we went against. A smarter robot could have taken better advantage of the physical strength of our design and collected cubes while also pinning down opponents. This was a fun and challenging experience, and I feel that I have learned a lot from it.
